## The Three Volumes

| Volume | Question | Status | Journey Chapters |
|--------|----------|--------|-----------------|
| **I** | Does God exist? | **COMPLETE** (v2.2.22) | Horizon → Signal (7 unique) + Resonance + Transmission + Conclusion |
| **II** | Which God? One or many? | **PLANNED** | ~6–8 new chapters per persona |
| **III** | Why Islam? Why not others? | **PLANNED** | ~6–8 new chapters per persona |

The argument flows as a single continuous journey: Volume I establishes theism from evidence alone (no scripture, no tradition). Volume II narrows from generic theism to monotheism and identifies which monotheistic tradition has the strongest rational and textual case. Volume III addresses Islam-specific objections and demonstrates why Islam, specifically, is the most compelling answer.

## Volume II: Which God? One or Many? — PLANNED

### Core Question

God exists (established in Volume I). But *which* God? Is the Creator personal or impersonal? One or many? Has this Creator communicated? If so, which tradition carries the authentic message? The answer, arrived at through evidence and reason, points to monotheism — and specifically to the Islamic conception of God (Tawhid).

### Proposed Chapter Structure (per persona)

Volume II adds ~6–8 new chapters to each persona's journey, continuing from the Transmission bridge. These follow the same pattern: persona-specific framing, shared argument structure, 6 reflection choices per chapter.

| Chapter | Working Title | Domain | Core Argument |
|---------|--------------|--------|---------------|
| **Criterion** | What authentic revelation would look like | Epistemology of Revelation | Establish falsifiable criteria *before* examining any tradition: internal consistency, historical preservation, universal address, falsifiable claims, moral coherence, literary uniqueness. |
| **Convergence** | The case against polytheism | Philosophical Theology | Multiple gods create logical problems: who arbitrates between them? The unity of natural law points to a single will. The philosophical argument for monotheism (Tawhid). |
| **Preservation** | Which text survived transmission? | Textual History | Comparative textual preservation: the Quran's documented chain of transmission vs the textual histories of the Bible, Vedas, and other scriptures. The Birmingham manuscript, the Sana'a palimpsest, the standardisation under Uthman. |
| **Coherence** | Which message is internally consistent? | Theology & Logic | The Quran's theological coherence: no councils, no redactions, no evolving doctrine. Contrast with the development of Trinitarian theology (Nicaea, Chalcedon). The logical problems with the Trinity examined philosophically. |
| **Address** | Which message addresses all of humanity? | Universalism | Does the tradition claim universal address? Judaism is ethnically bounded. Christianity's universalism came through Paul's revision of Jewish law. Islam's claim is explicitly universal from the outset. The Quran addresses "O humanity" (يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ). |
| **Character** | The Prophet as evidence | Prophetic Biography | Muhammad's character examined on the historical record — not hagiography. The Meccan period: 13 years of persecution without retaliation. The Medinan constitution. The farewell sermon. The conduct that enemies acknowledged. |
| **Verification** | The Quran's falsifiable claims | Textual Evidence | The Quran invites verification: "Will they not then reflect on the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than God, they would have found in it many inconsistencies" (4:82). Historical, scientific, and linguistic claims that can be tested. |
| **Reckoning** | Where the evidence lands | Synthesis | The cumulative case: the tradition that best satisfies every criterion established at the start of Volume II is Islam. Not by assertion — by examination. |

### Primary Audience & Scope

**This site is built for atheists.** Specifically: people who do not believe God exists — ex-Muslims who left for intellectual reasons, never-Muslims who dismiss religion entirely, and sceptics who demand evidence. The "spyrozaurous" archetype: science-forward, confrontational, demands empirical proof, dismisses all religion as human-created delusion.

All 14 personas are variations of non-belief. The entire Volume I argument is built for people who start from zero — no God, no scripture, no tradition. That is the audience.

**Other religions are out of scope.** This is not a comparative religion site. Volumes II and III may briefly address "why Islam and not Christianity/Hinduism/Buddhism?" when the argument naturally requires it (the Coherence chapter on Tawhid vs Trinity, the Address chapter on universality), but these are passing comparisons in service of the Islamic case — not standalone deep dives. If a reader has left Islam for Christianity specifically, that is a different problem requiring different content, and it is secondary to the core mission: making the case to those who believe in nothing.

## Volume III: Why Islam? Why Not Others? — PLANNED

### Core Question

Islam is the strongest candidate (established in Volume II). But what about the hardest objections? The violence, the law, the specific historical claims that trouble honest inquirers? Volume III addresses every major objection directly — not with apologetic deflection, but with honest engagement.

### Proposed Chapter Structure (per persona)

| Chapter | Working Title | Domain | Core Argument |
|---------|--------------|--------|---------------|
| **Examination** | The objections that deserve answers | Introduction | Names the hardest objections honestly. Promises direct engagement, not evasion. |
| **Context** | The historical world the Quran entered | History | 7th-century Arabia: what existed before Islam. Tribal law, female infanticide, slavery as norm. The revelation arrived into a specific context — and changed it directionally. |
| **Conduct** | Violence, war, and the Prophetic record | History & Ethics | Early Islamic warfare examined honestly. Banu Qurayza. The Meccan conquest (bloodless). Rules of engagement (no women, children, crops, monks). Comparison with contemporaries. |
| **Justice** | Women, law, and divine intent | Islamic Law | Women's rights in Islamic law: inheritance, testimony, marriage. The trajectory argument — Islam moved the needle dramatically from 7th-century norms. Where the tradition needs honest reckoning. |
| **Liberty** | Apostasy, freedom, and the tradition | Religious Freedom | The hadith "kill him who changes his religion" — its chain, context, scholarly dispute. The Quranic principle "no compulsion in religion." The political history of apostasy law vs the theological principle. |
| **Complexity** | The hard passages and what they mean | Quranic Hermeneutics | Abrogation, Meccan vs Medinan, the hermeneutical tradition. The Quran is not a flat text — it has layers, contexts, and a sophisticated interpretive tradition. |
| **Modernity** | Islam in the modern world | Contemporary | Evolution, science, democracy, human rights. Where Islamic thought has engaged modernity productively and where tensions remain. Honest about both. |
| **Invitation** | The door is open | Conclusion | Not a conversion call — an honest invitation. The evidence has been presented. The objections have been addressed. The rest is between the reader and God. |

### Other Tradition Comparatives (Volume II)

Ex-Muslims don't only land on atheism or Christianity. Volume II's Convergence and Coherence chapters must address the full landscape:

**Hinduism:**
- Pull factor: spiritual richness, meditation, karma as "fairer" than eternal hell, no single authority to rebel against
- Key comparison: polytheism vs monotheism (Tawhid), Vedantic monism vs Islamic theism, caste system vs universal address
- Audience: South Asian ex-Muslims, Western seekers drawn to yoga/meditation culture
- Planned article: "Hinduism's God Problem: Monism, Polytheism, or Neither?"

**Buddhism:**
- Pull factor: no God required, mindfulness/meditation appeal, "scientific" reputation, escape from hell/judgment theology
- Key comparison: Buddhism's silence on God vs Islam's explicit theism, the Buddhist suffering framework (dukkha) vs Islamic theodicy, nirvana vs akhirah
- Audience: Southeast Asian ex-Muslims (Thailand, Myanmar border communities), Western converts to secular Buddhism
- Planned article: "Buddhism and the God Question: What the Buddha Actually Said"

**Zoroastrianism:**
- Pull factor: "original monotheism," dualism as an answer to evil, Persian cultural identity for Iranian ex-Muslims
- Key comparison: Ahura Mazda vs Allah, dualism vs Tawhid (evil as a separate force vs evil as permitted by one God), fire worship allegations vs actual Zoroastrian theology
- Audience: Iranian ex-Muslims, those interested in pre-Islamic Persian identity
- Planned article: "Zoroastrianism: The Monotheism That Islam Acknowledges"

**General comparative article needed:**
- "You Left Islam — But Did You Compare, or Just Switch?" — a framework article that applies to ANY tradition switch, establishing the criteria before examining specific alternatives

All of these slot into Volume II's chapter structure:
- **Convergence** chapter addresses polytheism (Hinduism) and non-theism (Buddhism)
- **Coherence** chapter addresses Trinitarian theology (Christianity) and dualism (Zoroastrianism)
- **Address** chapter addresses ethnic boundedness (Hinduism's caste, Judaism's peoplehood) vs Islam's universal claim

## Notes for Future Developers

1. **Each journey file is self-contained HTML** — all CSS, JS, and content in one file. This was a deliberate choice for offline capability and CDN-free distribution. The tradeoff is file size (currently 140–180KB each; will grow to ~400KB+ with three volumes). Lazy-loading individual volumes within each file may be needed.

2. **The quiz currently routes to Volume I**. Volume II routing could work two ways: (a) automatic continuation after Volume I conclusion, or (b) a separate "Volume II readiness" assessment that appears after Volume I completion. Option (a) is simpler; option (b) allows re-calibration based on where the reader actually landed after Volume I.

3. **The True Muslim journey does NOT participate in Volumes II/III**. It is a pastoral/equipping path, not an apologetics path. It could potentially have its own Volume II ("Advanced Engagement") covering how to discuss comparative religion, but this is a separate design decision.

4. **Arabic citations** should continue to use Uthmanic rasm throughout all volumes. Volume II will need significantly more Quranic citations (preservation, literary argument, falsifiability). Volume III will need both Quran and hadith citations for the historical/legal chapters.

5. **The scoring matrix** will likely need expansion for Volume II — a user who completes Volume I as an agnostic and is now open to theism may need different Volume II framing than one who completed it as a new-atheist who reluctantly accepted the convergence. Post-Volume-I scoring signals could include: which reflection choices they selected, how far they progressed before completing, and whether they engaged with go-deeper articles.

6. **See the Technical Upgrade Guide** section below for WordPress theme upgrade procedures, database migration notes, and technical deployment instructions.

## Technical Upgrade Guide

*(Merged from UPGRADING-TECHNICAL.md)*

### Theme Core Upgrade

```
1. Back up /wp-content/themes/compelling-evidence/
2. Upload new version via Appearance → Themes → Add New → Upload Theme
3. Confirm replacement when prompted
4. Activate the updated theme
5. Visit any admin page (migration + content sync run automatically)
6. Settings → Permalinks → Save Changes
```

Three auto-run modules fire on `admin_init` (each runs once, flagged by option):

| Module | Flag | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `ce-migration-2-2-0.php` | `ce_category_migration_2_2_0` | Creates 10 taxonomy terms, remaps 60+ articles, renames slugs, deletes old terms |
| `ce-content-sync.php` | `ce_content_sync_2_2_8` | Updates all article content from data files, creates missing articles |
| `ce-secondary-pages.php` | `ce_secondary_pages_created` | Creates About, Editorial, Privacy, Contact, Ask a Question pages |

To force a re-run: delete the corresponding option from `wp_options` table or via WP-CLI:
```bash
wp option delete ce_content_sync_2_2_8
```

### Journey HTML Files

Journey files can be updated independently — no PHP changes or permalink flush needed. Just replace the file in `/journeys/` and clear cache.

### Quiz

Replace `/journeys/quiz.html` directly. If scoring weights change between versions, existing localStorage results may need invalidation.

### CSS Updates

Replace `/assets/css/main.css`. Increment `Version:` in `style.css` to invalidate browser cache. Journey pages embed their own CSS and are unaffected by main.css changes.

### Parent Theme (Twenty Twenty-Five)

The child theme uses only standard WordPress hooks — no parent-specific dependencies. After a major parent theme update, verify the front page and article rendering. Journey and quiz pages are fully isolated.

### Database

No custom tables. Data stored in `wp_posts`, `wp_postmeta`, `wp_terms`, `wp_term_taxonomy`, `wp_term_relationships`, and `wp_options` (for migration flags). No manual database migration needed — the auto-run modules handle everything.

### Rollback

Restore theme folder from backup, activate, and flush permalinks. localStorage progress and WP user meta are unaffected by theme rollback.
